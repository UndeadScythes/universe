\section{Language}
\label{sec:language}
\commentary{
    \emph{Universe} is an attempt to construct a mathematical description of reality from the smallest possible foundations.
    
    We begin with a formal language: a collection of symbols and rules from which more complicated mathematical statements can be constructed. At this stage, the symbols have no assumed mathematical interpretation.
}

\subsection{Primitive symbols}
\begin{foundation}
    \[
        \in,\quad \forall,\quad \nor
    \]
\end{foundation}
% ...and of course some method of creating infinite variable placeholders: o, o', o'', o''' etc.

\subsection{Formation rules}
\begin{foundation}
    \begin{align*}
        &{\in}ab \\
        &{\forall}aP \\
        &{\nor}PQ
    \end{align*}    
\end{foundation}
% Implicit use of lower case for variables and upper case for formulae.

\subsection{Notational conventions}
%\subsubsection{Infix notation}
\begin{notation}
    \begin{align*}
        a \in b &\coloneqq {\in}ab \\
        \forall a: P &\coloneqq {\forall}aP \\
        P \nor Q &\coloneqq {\nor}PQ
    \end{align*}
\end{notation}
% This means much of the punctuation used is intuitive, but we can still burn down to minimal alphabet.

\commentary{
    \textbf{Metasyntactic conventions.} Variables, formulas, grouping, substitution, and related syntactic operations are understood in their conventional mathematical sense. Their explicit formalisation is deferred.
}

\commentary{
    These definitions establish the syntax and notational conventions used throughout \emph{Universe}. They do not yet establish which statements are true; that requires axioms and rules of inference.
}
